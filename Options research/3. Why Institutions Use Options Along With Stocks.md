# Module 3 — Why Institutions Use Options Along With Stocks

## Research Question

If institutions can already express bullish or bearish views by trading stocks, why do they also trade options?

---

# Core Concepts

## 1. Institutions optimize exposure, not holdings.

Retail investors often think in terms of holdings:

> "What should I buy?"

Institutions usually think differently:

> "What exposure do I want?"

Stocks, options, futures and swaps are simply different tools for constructing the desired exposure.

Holding is the implementation.

Exposure is the objective.

---

## 2. Investment View and Risk Management are independent.

An institution may have exactly the same market view but choose completely different portfolios.

Example:

Fund A and Fund B are both bullish on NVDA.

Fund A buys stock.

Fund B buys stock and purchases protective puts.

Both funds have the same investment conviction.

However, they have different downside risk.

Therefore,

Investment View ≠ Portfolio Construction.

---

## 3. Protective Put

Research Question:

Why would an institution buy a put while already holding the stock?

Answer:

Selling the stock removes both downside risk and upside potential.

Buying a put allows the institution to

- maintain long-term stock exposure
- reduce downside risk
- protect against temporary uncertainty

Examples include

- earnings announcements
- macroeconomic events
- temporary market uncertainty

The institution has not changed its opinion.

It has only changed its risk profile.

---

## 4. Covered Call

Research Question:

Why would an institution sell call options while already owning the stock?

Answer:

Covered calls generate option premium income.

The institution voluntarily gives up part of the future upside in exchange for immediate and predictable cash flow.

This strategy is commonly used by

- pension funds
- income-oriented portfolios
- long-term investors

Again,

the institution has not necessarily become bearish.

It has changed the payoff profile of the portfolio.

---

## 5. Holdings and Exposure are different.

Two institutions may report identical stock holdings.

However,

one institution may also own puts,

sell covered calls,

or hold other option positions.

Their reported holdings are identical.

Their economic exposure is not.

Therefore,

Holding ≠ Exposure.

---

## 6. Not all call options represent the same stock exposure.

Suppose Apple trades at $200.

A deep ITM call with strike 100 behaves almost like stock.

A far OTM call with strike 300 behaves very differently.

Therefore,

simply counting option contracts provides very little information.

The important question is

> How much stock exposure does the option position actually represent?

---

## 7. Delta

Economic interpretation:

Delta measures how much an option behaves like the underlying stock.

A deep ITM call has high delta.

A far OTM call has low delta.

For our research,

Delta can be interpreted as

> the conversion factor between option positions and stock exposure.

---

## 8. Gamma

Gamma explains why dealer hedging changes over time.

As the stock price moves,

the option's delta changes.

Therefore,

dealers must continuously adjust their hedge.

This creates additional buying or selling in the underlying stock.

Economic interpretation:

Gamma measures how quickly option exposure changes.

Market interpretation:

Gamma determines how frequently dealers must rebalance their stock hedge.

---

## 9. How Option Market Influences Stock Market

Institution buys options

↓

Dealer takes the opposite side

↓

Dealer's exposure changes

↓

Dealer hedges using the underlying stock

↓

Stock order flow changes

↓

Observed stock market behaviour changes

Therefore,

stock market activity may sometimes originate from option market activity rather than stock investors themselves.

---

# Key Insights

1. Institutions optimize exposure rather than holdings.

2. Options are primarily risk-management tools rather than directional bets.

3. Protective puts change risk without changing investment conviction.

4. Covered calls exchange future upside for immediate income.

5. Holding and exposure are fundamentally different concepts.

6. Delta measures effective stock exposure.

7. Gamma explains why dealer hedging changes over time.

8. Option markets can indirectly influence stock markets through dealer hedging.

# Research Reflection

Originally,

institutional positioning was viewed mainly through stock holdings.

After studying institutional option usage,

it becomes clear that

economic exposure and reported holdings are not equivalent.

Option strategies allow institutions to reshape portfolio risk while leaving stock holdings largely unchanged.

Furthermore,

option market activity may influence the stock market itself through dealer hedging.

Therefore,

stock market observations should not always be interpreted as originating solely from stock investors.
