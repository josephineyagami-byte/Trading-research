# Module 3 — Why Institutions Use Options Along With Stocks

## Section 3.1 — Protective Put

### Research Question

Why would an institution that already owns a stock buy a put option instead of simply selling the stock?

---

## Core Concepts

Owning stock and buying a put does not indicate a change in market view.

Instead, it changes the portfolio's risk profile.

The institution remains long the stock while limiting downside risk.

---

## Key Insight

Institutions generally separate

- investment conviction

from

- risk management.

A portfolio manager may remain bullish while simultaneously reducing downside exposure.

Therefore,

options are primarily tools for managing risk rather than changing investment views.

---

## Why Not Simply Sell The Stock?

Selling the stock removes both

- downside risk

and

- upside potential.

Buying a put allows the institution to

- maintain long-term exposure

while

- limiting losses if adverse events occur.

Examples include

- earnings announcements
- macro events
- temporary uncertainty

---

## Connection to Our Project

Our current model estimates institutional activity primarily from stock market observations.

However, an institution may significantly modify its effective exposure without changing its stock holdings.

Therefore,

stock holdings alone do not fully describe institutional positioning.

Current project does not model option positions.

Nevertheless, understanding this distinction is important when interpreting institutional behaviour.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Institutions optimise exposure rather than holdings.

2. Options modify risk without necessarily changing market conviction.

3. Protective puts preserve upside while limiting downside.

4. Holding and exposure are not equivalent concepts.
