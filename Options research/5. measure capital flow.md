# Module 5 — Measuring Capital Flow

## Research Question

How should "money flow" be defined, measured and interpreted in institutional trading research?

Rather than asking

> "Is money flowing into a stock?"

this module asks

> "What exactly do researchers mean by money flow?"

---

# Core Concepts

## 1. Money Does Not Literally Flow Into A Stock

In the secondary market,

every buyer has a seller.

Cash simply transfers between market participants.

Therefore,

money does not physically enter a stock.

What actually changes is

- ownership
- portfolio allocation
- investor composition.

---

## 2. Capital Allocation

The phrase

> "Money flowed into NVIDIA"

usually means

capital allocation shifted toward NVIDIA.

It does **not** mean NVIDIA itself received cash.

Capital allocation is therefore more fundamental than the popular expression "money flow".

---

## 3. Money Flow Depends On Definition

Different research questions require different definitions.

Examples include

- Tick Rule Money Flow
- Order Flow
- OFI
- Large Trade Flow
- ETF Flow
- Mutual Fund Flow
- 13F Holdings Change

These are **not** equivalent.

Each measures a different aspect of market behaviour.

---

## 4. Hidden Variables

The quantities researchers truly care about are usually unobservable.

Examples

- Institutional Position
- Institutional Demand
- Capital Allocation
- Smart Money Activity

These are hidden economic states.

Research therefore relies on observable market data to infer them.

---

## 5. Observable Evidence

Observable variables include

- Main Flow
- OFI
- VWAP Deviation
- Price Impact
- Relative Volume

These variables are **not** institutional positions.

They are pieces of evidence that may reflect institutional activity.

---

## 6. Proxy Variables

A proxy is an observable variable that contains information about a hidden variable.

A good proxy should satisfy four properties:

### Economic mechanism

There should be a clear economic explanation linking the proxy to the hidden state.

### Information content

The proxy should genuinely contain information about the hidden variable.

### Availability

The data should be obtainable at the required frequency.

### Incremental information

The proxy should contribute information beyond existing variables.

---

## 7. Large Trades As A Proxy

Large trades do not equal institutional trading.

Instead,

large trades are statistically more likely to involve institutions than very small trades.

Therefore,

Large Trade is a probabilistic proxy rather than direct observation.

---

## 8. Correlation Is Not Information

High correlation alone does not imply usefulness.

Two features may both correlate strongly with the target while providing almost identical information.

Research should ask

> Does this feature contribute new information?

rather than

> Does this feature have a high correlation?

---

## 9. Redundant vs Complementary Information

Redundant features

observe essentially the same market mechanism.

Complementary features

observe different mechanisms.

Examples

OFI

↓

Order Book Dynamics

Main Flow

↓

Large Trade Activity

Dealer Gamma

↓

Option Market Hedging

Although all three may relate to institutional activity,

they originate from different economic mechanisms.

---

## 10. Information Structure

Different stocks may not react to the same features in the same way.

Possible reasons include

- Liquidity
- Market Capitalization
- Sector
- Volatility
- Market Microstructure

This introduces the concept of **heterogeneity**.

Different groups of stocks may require different feature responses.

---

## 11. Response-Based Grouping

Instead of assuming every stock shares the same feature-response relationship,

research may first estimate each stock's response profile,

then group stocks with similar response structures.

This approach is data-driven.

However,

economic interpretation should still be examined afterwards by comparing the resulting groups with known characteristics such as liquidity, market capitalization or sector.

---

## 12. Observable Does Not Equal Truth

Observable market variables are never the hidden economic process itself.

Example

Institution Position

↓

Institution Trading

↓

Observable Market Features

↓

Model

↓

Estimated Institution Position

The model performs statistical inference rather than direct observation.

# Connection To Our Project

The current project estimates institutional position changes using observable stock market features.

Current observable evidence includes

- Main Flow
- OFI
- VWAP Deviation
- Relative Volume
- Price Impact

These variables are not institutional positions.

Instead,

they provide multiple imperfect observations of hidden institutional behaviour.

The HMM combines these observations to estimate an underlying hidden state.

---

# Key Insights

1. Money flow is a definition rather than a directly observable quantity.

2. Capital allocation is more fundamental than the phrase "money flowing into a stock."

3. Observable market features are evidence, not truth.

4. Every feature is a proxy with limitations.

5. Good features should provide incremental information.

6. Feature engineering is fundamentally evidence engineering.

7. Different stocks may exhibit different response structures.

8. Stock grouping should ideally combine empirical evidence with economic interpretation.

9. Institutional position estimation is fundamentally an inference problem.

---

# Research Reflection

The objective of the project is not to identify institutions directly.

Instead,

the objective is to combine multiple observable market signals,

each containing partial information,

to infer an unobservable institutional state.

This transforms feature engineering into an evidence integration problem.
