# Module 4 — Reading the Option Market

## Research Question

How should option market information be interpreted if our goal is to better understand institutional positioning?

This module does **not** study options from a trading perspective.

Instead, it studies how option market information may help explain stock market behaviour.

---

# Core Concepts

## 1. Trading Activity vs Outstanding Position

Option Volume and Open Interest describe different aspects of the market.

Volume measures

- trading activity
- contracts exchanged today

Open Interest measures

- outstanding positions
- contracts that remain open after today's trading

Therefore,

Volume answers

> "How active was today's trading?"

Open Interest answers

> "How much option exposure currently exists?"

---

## 2. High Volume Does Not Necessarily Mean New Positions

Large trading volume alone is insufficient.

Examples

High Volume + Unchanged OI

→ Existing positions changing hands.

High Volume + Increasing OI

→ New positions likely created.

High Volume + Falling OI

→ Existing positions likely closed.

Therefore,

Volume should always be interpreted together with Open Interest.

---

## 3. Strike Price Matters

Open Interest is not equally important across all strikes.

Options close to the current stock price generally carry much larger effective stock exposure than deep out-of-the-money contracts.

Therefore,

the distribution of Open Interest is often more informative than its total amount.

---

## 4. Expiration Matters

Options with different maturities behave differently.

Near-term options generally require more frequent dealer hedge adjustments.

Long-dated options typically produce much slower hedge adjustments.

Therefore,

Expiration is an essential dimension when analysing option markets.

---

## 5. Delta

Economic interpretation:

Delta measures how much an option behaves like the underlying stock.

Research interpretation:

Delta converts option positions into stock-equivalent exposure.

Therefore,

option contracts should not be counted equally.

They should be interpreted through their effective stock exposure.

---

## 6. Dealer Hedging

Institutions trade options.

Dealers take the opposite side.

Dealers then hedge using the underlying stock.

Therefore,

option trading influences stock markets indirectly through dealer hedging.

Dealer trading is primarily driven by inventory risk management rather than investment opinions.

---

## 7. Gamma

Dealer hedging is dynamic.

As stock prices move,

option delta changes.

Dealers therefore continuously adjust their stock hedge.

Gamma determines how rapidly this hedge must change.

Economic interpretation:

Gamma measures the sensitivity of stock exposure itself.

Market interpretation:

Gamma determines how frequently dealers need to rebalance their stock inventory.

---

## 8. Positive Feedback

Large option positioning can produce a feedback loop.

Institution buys options

↓

Dealer sells options

↓

Dealer buys stock to hedge

↓

Stock price rises

↓

Delta increases

↓

Dealer buys additional stock

↓

Stock price rises further

Dealer does not intentionally push prices.

The feedback loop is a natural consequence of dynamic hedging.

---

# Reading Option Markets

When analysing option markets, avoid asking

> "What happened today?"

Instead ask

> "How has market exposure changed?"

Research should focus on

- Position
- Exposure
- Dealer Hedging

rather than simply

- Trading Volume
- Number of Contracts

---

# Practical Interpretation

Professional option analysis rarely relies on a single metric.

Instead,

Open Interest

+

Strike

+

Expiration

+

Delta

are interpreted together.

The objective is to understand

where meaningful exposure exists,

rather than where trading activity is simply high.

# Key Insights

1. Volume measures trading activity.

2. Open Interest measures outstanding positions.

3. Strike and Expiration determine the economic importance of option positions.

4. Delta measures effective stock exposure.

5. Dealer hedging connects option markets to stock markets.

6. Gamma explains why dealer hedging changes dynamically.

7. Option market data should be interpreted through its impact on stock market behaviour.

---

# Research Reflection

The objective is not to model the option market itself.

Instead,

the objective is to understand how option market activity influences observable stock market behaviour.

Option data therefore represents another potential source of information about institutional positioning rather than a separate research topic.
